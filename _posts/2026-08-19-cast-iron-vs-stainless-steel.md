# Cast Iron vs Stainless Steel Pans: Which Should You Buy?

It's the oldest debate in home kitchens. The honest answer: **you probably want both** — but if you're buying just one, here's how to choose.

## The short version

- **Cast iron** — unbeatable sear and heat retention, cheap, lasts forever. Heavy and needs seasoning.
- **Stainless steel** — responsive, light, dishwasher-safe, great for sauces and deglazing. Pricier and food sticks if you don't learn the technique.

## Cast iron — best for searing

Cast iron holds heat like nothing else, giving steaks and veggies a deep, even crust. It's nearly indestructible and costs little.

- **Best for:** searing meat, frying, baking (cornbread, skillet dishes).
- **Price:** ~$25–50 (a Lodge skillet lasts a lifetime).
- **Trade-off:** heavy; needs seasoning; reacts with acidic sauces.

## Stainless steel — best all-rounder

Stainless (tri-ply like All-Clad) is responsive — it heats and cools fast, and its flat surface is perfect for deglazing into pan sauces.

- **Best for:** sauces, sautéing, everyday cooking, oven-to-stove.
- **Price:** ~$100–200 for a good tri-ply skillet.
- **Trade-off:** food sticks until you learn heat + oil technique; pricier.

## The verdict

- **Love a perfect sear, cook meat often?** Cast iron (start with a Lodge).
- **Cook sauces, want one versatile pan?** Stainless steel.
- **Best advice:** get a cheap cast iron AND a good stainless — they complement each other perfectly.
